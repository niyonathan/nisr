# Yield model results

Generated by `analysis/yield_model.py` from NISR AHS 2024 microdata.

## Data

- 57,506 crop-on-plot records with a valid yield, 20 crops with at least 300 records each.
- Outcome: log yield (kg/ha). Yield = harvest ÷ (plot area × crop's share of the plot).

## 1. Which practices go with higher yields?

One regression per crop: log yield on each practice, controlling for plot size, crop share, number of crops on the plot, soil threats, household head sex and household size, with district and season fixed effects. Standard errors are clustered by survey cluster. Effects are the % difference in yield between plots that use the practice and similar plots that don't.

These are **associations** in survey data. Farmers who use fertilizer may also differ in ways the survey does not record (soil quality, skills), so the numbers show where yields are higher, not a guaranteed result of adopting a practice.

**Reading the irrigation results.** Only about 3% of modelled plots are irrigated, mostly Season C vegetables and marshland rice, so irrigated and rain-fed plots of the same crop are not comparable. The negative coefficients for beans and rice reflect where and when irrigation is used, not irrigation lowering yields. The platform therefore treats irrigation as a planning question (where water is missing or unaffordable) rather than a yield-effect claim.

## 2. Yield predictor

Gradient-boosted trees (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`) on log yield, one model for all 20 crops, with 32 features: crop, district, season, seed, fertilizer type and rate, pesticide, irrigation status, soil conservation, land tenure, and household characteristics. Every tree leaf summarises at least 50 plots.

Validated with 5-fold cross-validation grouped by survey cluster, against a benchmark that predicts the median yield of the same crop in the same district.

| | R² (log yield) | Median absolute error |
|---|---:|---:|
| Benchmark: crop × district median | 0.43 | 55% |
| Yield predictor | 0.54 | 51% |

The model beats the benchmark for all 20 crops (within-crop R² 0.09–0.31). Single-plot predictions remain uncertain, because weather, pests and soil quality are not in the survey, so the app presents expected ranges and comparisons with similar farms, not exact forecasts.

## 3. Input scenario

For each district and crop, the predictor estimates average yield if plots lacking improved seed or inorganic fertilizer adopted them, at the median fertilizer rate of users of that crop. An input is included only for crops where at least 5% of plots (and 30+) already use it, so the model never borrows patterns from other crops. This is what the model expects for similar plots, not a promised gain.
